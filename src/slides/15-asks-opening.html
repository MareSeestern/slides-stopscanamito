  • Back from the tech section. Provenance matured a lot, and the usability gap is what remains. Three limits, all things Amito has to work around.
  • Forged. Buchanan's frog image is AI-generated and carries a Pixel Camera signature saying it is an unedited photo. Pixel 8a and 9a, rooted or fault-injected, the key used as a signing oracle and never extracted. Not Pixel 10. It is a blog post, not peer-reviewed.
  • JPEG Trust, on the Forged card. A trust profile that trusts the Pixel signer would rate Buchanan's image as a camera capture. That is our reading of how a profile treats a trusted signer. Demirtas et al. do not discuss this case.
  • If asked for more. Nikon suspended content credentials on the Z6III in Sep 2025 after a signing flaw and invalidated every certificate (DPReview). Photographing a synthetic image on a screen with a signing camera yields a valid signature (Park et al., USENIX Security 2025). Unsigned IPTC and Exif fields can be rewritten by anyone.
  • Stripped. IPTC's own Metawatch, October 2026 run. 11% of 6,580 lead photos from 425 news publishers carried any IPTC metadata, and 7.2% carried credit or copyright. 27 had a C2PA manifest, all but one signed by an AI or design tool, none by a camera or a newsroom. Runs are monthly, so quote the October run. Platforms read different signals. Instagram reads Digital Source Type, LinkedIn reads C2PA.
  • Both ways, IPTC. Meta renamed Made with AI to AI info on 1 Jul 2024, after content with minor AI modifications such as retouching got the label. A forged or over-broad declaration can make a real photo look fake, the liar's dividend from the start of the talk. For JPEG Trust this card leaves the slide and stays in these notes.
  • JPEG Trust, third card. Authentic is not accurate. An authentic image with valid provenance can still carry the wrong event, time or place. Demirtas et al., SPIE 2026 manuscript. The Factuality Trust Indicator is their proposal, not part of the standard.
