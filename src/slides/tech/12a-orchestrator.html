The numbers show one route. The person's answer picks the first check, and the agent proposes the next.

  • Amito is an agent in a chat. It doesn't run a fixed pipeline, and the checks have no fixed order. The person's answer to its first question picks the first check. The pixels when something looks off, the place when they say where it was taken, the credentials otherwise. Then it runs one check at a time, explains it, and proposes the next. The person can also ask for a check by name, like who posted it.
  • Beat 2. Around it are the tools, coloured by the STOP&SCAN step they serve. Source in cyan, Content in green, Alignment in pink. In the terms of authenticity, the Source tools answer provenance and authentication, who signed it and what happened to it. The detectors answer integrity, did the content change. The place, the weather and the records answer context. File metadata includes the IPTC Digital Source Type.
  • Beat 3. One route through them. A photo shared as real, with no other hint, so credentials first, then the pixels, then the caption, then the story against the photo's own records. Every result goes into the Amito trust report, where a trust profile checks its rules in order, strongest evidence first, so a signed declaration outweighs a detector score.
  • The rules decide the verdict, not the AI model. The model only runs the conversation and picks the checks, so OpenAI, Claude or a local model give the same verdict on the same evidence. With a local model the images can stay on the organisation's own computer.
